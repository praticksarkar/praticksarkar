Research

Axion-like particles and dark matter, told through three observables

The strong CP problem motivates the Peccei–Quinn mechanism, and with it a light pseudoscalar — the axion, or more generally an axion-like particle (ALP). My thesis asks where that particle, or the dark matter it may partly compose, would leave a trace in things we can already observe: a black hole's photon ring, a galaxy's spectrum, and the stochastic hum of gravitational waves.

Part I

ALP detection via black hole photon rings

The Event Horizon Telescope's image of M87* shows a photon ring — light that has orbited the black hole before escaping toward us. An ALP background along that path induces a birefringence-like effect described by the Euler–Heisenberg Lagrangian, shifting the ring's polarization in a way that depends on the ALP–photon coupling. I model this effect and derive constraints from the observed ring properties.

Published — JCAP 11(2023)099

Part II

eV-scale ALP dark matter from M87 galaxy spectroscopy

If dark matter is composed of eV-scale ALPs, its density profile around M87 (modeled with NFW, Burkert, and spike profiles) sets a local plasma frequency Δ_pl that governs photon–ALP conversion. Comparing predicted conversion against galaxy spectroscopic data, via the Raffelt–Stodolski formalism and a conversion factor CF, yields bounds on the ALP–photon coupling in this mass range.

Published — JCAP 05(2025)100

Part III

Ultralight dark matter in the gravitational-wave background

Ultralight dark matter (ULDM) behaves as a coherent wave described by the Schrödinger–Poisson equation, forming a soliton core at the center of galaxies — a possible resolution to the cusp–core problem. Self-coupling in the ULDM field alters dynamical friction and soliton dynamics, leaving a signature in the stochastic gravitational-wave background (SGWB) measurable by pulsar timing arrays: NANOGrav, EPTA, and PPTA.

arXiv:2504.19505
